Fischertechnik demonstrator

Setup

The factory stores, identifies, moves, processes and sorts workpieces. A robot transfers them between stations.

Local control: Turck PLC and HMI with CODESYS. The standard manual also describes a TXT controller, Raspberry Pi gateway and cloud services; those are not the default configuration of this installation.

Descriptions below combine the manufacturer’s component manual, local installation observations and the supplied knowledge graph. Use the source links to distinguish standard equipment from local observations.

Modules

XYZ robot / vacuum gripper (VGR)

Moves workpieces between the warehouse, delivery/RFID area and processing station. The three axes provide rotation, horizontal travel and vertical travel. A suction cup grips the workpiece.

Parts and fault context

Encoder motors and reference switches position the axes. A compressor, valve and coupled cylinders generate the vacuum for gripping.

Installation notes describe missed pickups, dropped workpieces and mispositioning. The exact root cause of the calibration problems remains uncertain.

High-bay warehouse (HBW)

Stores and retrieves workpiece carriers using a rack-handling mechanism and conveyor. Workpieces sit in carriers; detecting a carrier does not prove that it contains a workpiece.

Parts and fault context

Encoder and mini motors drive handling and transport. Buttons act as reference switches; phototransistors detect objects at transfer points.

Observed issues include incorrect colour arrangement, reversed or empty carriers, loose drive parts and missed pickup positions.

Multi-processing station (MPO)

Simulates processing with a furnace, transfer gripper, rotary table, milling stage and conveyor. A pneumatic ejector transfers the workpiece to the outgoing conveyor.

Parts and fault context

Light barriers detect transfers. Motors drive the slide, rotary table and conveyor; pneumatic components operate the gripper and ejector.

The installation notes describe loose pneumatic tubes and mechanical misalignment as causes of failed transfers. These observations are not a diagnosis of every processing failure.

Manual, p. 5Query problems and causesParts reference

Sorting line (SLD)

Moves workpieces past a colour sensor and uses pneumatic ejectors to separate them into three chutes. Light barriers monitor passage and storage locations.

Parts and fault context

A motor drives the belt. Pulse sensing relates ejection to belt movement. Solenoid valves control the cylinders.

The manual explains that ambient light, surface finish, sensor distance and threshold settings influence colour recognition. These are operating considerations, not all observed installation faults.

Delivery and RFID station

Provides the handover area for workpieces and reads their embedded tags. The local installation notes describe tags carrying colour information; the knowledge graph includes an RFID Station with the function “Read workpiece data”.

Parts and fault context

The standard manual describes input/output handling, colour recognition and an NFC reader. The supplied photo shows the complete local setup; no separate RFID close-up was provided.

The notes describe missing workpieces and colour mismatches becoming visible downstream, after an earlier handling or warehouse problem.

Control and monitoring

The local demonstrator uses a Turck PLC and HMI programmed with CODESYS. It does not use the manual’s default TXT/Raspberry Pi setup.

Parts and fault context

The graph lists a PLC, HMI, digital and analogue I/O, network interface and 24 V supply. Environmental sensing is described in the standard manual, but its connection and use in the local setup must be verified.

The notes describe lost signals from loose connectors, timeouts and incorrect process states. A detected signal or completed movement command does not always establish successful workpiece delivery.

Parts and sensors

Encoder motor

Drives an axis while generating pulses used to estimate motion. In this setup the PLC uses configured encoder counts; the installation notes report variation in the resulting positions.

Robot and warehouse

Manual, p. 3

Mini motor

Drives mechanisms such as conveyors and processing movements. Its presence does not imply continuous position measurement.

Warehouse, processing and sorting

Manual, p. 4

Button / reference switch

Signals that a moving part has reached a reference position. A missed switch can leave the controller without the expected reference signal.

Robot and moving modules

Manual, p. 3

Phototransistor / light barrier

Detects interruption of a light beam at a transfer point. It detects presence at that point; it does not identify the workpiece or always establish that a carrier is loaded.

Warehouse, processing and sorting

Manual, p. 4

Colour sensor

Classifies reflected light using thresholds. Distance, alignment, lighting and surface condition affect its readings.

Sorting and input/output station

Manual, p. 6

Compressor

Supplies compressed air to the pneumatic circuits. The local notes report no direct pressure or vacuum measurement.

Robot, processing and sorting

Manual, p. 3

Solenoid valve

Electrically switches the pneumatic air path to operate cylinders or the vacuum mechanism.

Pneumatic circuits

Manual, p. 3

Pneumatic cylinder

Converts air pressure into motion, for example for an ejector. Coupled cylinders are also used in the robot’s vacuum-generation mechanism.

Robot, processing and sorting

Manual, p. 3

Suction cup

Holds a workpiece when a vacuum seal is established. Leakage or poor alignment can prevent a seal and lead to failed pickups.

Environmental sensor

The standard manual describes temperature, humidity, air pressure and air-quality sensing, plus a separate brightness sensor. Availability and wiring in the Turck configuration are not established by the supplied notes.
